# Building Evidence Instead of Feelings

## Objective
Shift the reader away from mood-based self-evaluation and toward visible evidence of skill, recovery, and contribution.

## Key Ideas
- Feelings are unstable metrics.
- Evidence logs make invisible progress visible again.
- Confidence becomes sturdier when it is tied to facts.

## Draft
If you only ask yourself whether you feel confident, you will get unreliable data.

Mood changes quickly.

Stress distorts memory.

Bad days rewrite perception.

A better approach is to build evidence.

Track solved problems.

Track systems you understand better now.

Track situations that once overwhelmed you but no longer do.

Confidence should come from evidence, not mood.

That shift matters because evidence remains true even when your feelings temporarily crash.

REALITY CHECK:
You do not need to feel strong to have proof that you are improving.

DEBUG NOTE:
The brain forgets progress. Records do not.
