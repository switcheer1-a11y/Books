# The Cognitive Bugs Behind Imposter Syndrome

## Objective
Translate common cognitive distortions into developer-native mental debugging patterns so readers can recognize the bugs in their own thinking.

## Key Ideas
- Overgeneralization turns one mistake into an identity statement.
- Perfectionism treats anything short of flawless as failure.
- Comparison distortion ignores invisible context.
- Catastrophizing predicts collapse from normal setbacks.

## Draft
Imposter syndrome often feels mysterious.

But much of it becomes easier to understand when you treat it like a recurring bug pattern.

The first bug is overgeneralization.

One failed interview, one awkward meeting, one broken deployment becomes proof that you are not good enough.

The second bug is perfectionism.

You do not ask whether the work was useful.

You ask whether it was flawless.

The third bug is comparison distortion.

You compare your rough internal state to someone else’s polished external output.

The fourth bug is catastrophizing.

A normal problem gets mentally compiled into a total career failure.

DEBUG NOTE:
A distorted conclusion can feel emotionally true while still being logically broken.

PATTERN:
Mistake → self-judgment → overthinking → exhaustion
