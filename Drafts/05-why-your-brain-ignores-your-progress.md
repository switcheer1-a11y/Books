# Why Your Brain Ignores Your Progress

## Objective
Help the reader understand why achievements often lose emotional weight quickly, making growth feel invisible.

## Key Ideas
- Negativity bias makes problems stand out more than progress.
- Adaptation causes yesterday’s growth to feel normal today.
- Identity lags behind actual competence.

## Draft
One of the most frustrating parts of progress is that it often stops feeling like progress almost immediately.

The thing that once intimidated you becomes normal.

Then the brain moves on.

It highlights the next gap, the next weakness, the next thing you still cannot do.

This creates a strange experience.

You improve, but you do not feel improved.

That does not mean the growth was fake.

It means the mind is much faster at updating problems than identity.

Your brain updates problems faster than identity.

REALITY CHECK:
If a task now feels ordinary, that may be proof that you learned it.

DEBUG NOTE:
Competence often becomes emotionally invisible once it becomes familiar.
